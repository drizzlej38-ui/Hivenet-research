# Open-Ended Artificial Ecosystem: Simulation Protocol

**Status:** Proposed experiment; no implementation or emergent result is claimed  
**Version:** 0.1  
**Date:** 2026-11-26

## 1. Research question

What organizational structures, behaviors, and capabilities, if any, arise when a computational ecosystem is provided conditions for operation and internal development, but no assigned intelligence objective, preferred social structure, or prescribed developmental destination?

The experiment must admit uninteresting, inefficient, unstable, fragmented, or unsuccessful outcomes.

## 2. Hypothesis and null hypothesis

**H-ECO-01:** Under some initial conditions and environmental dynamics, interacting computational components may develop persistent organization, specialization, coordination, or problem-solving not explicitly prescribed.

**H0-ECO-01:** Apparent organization or problem-solving can be explained by initial rules, engineered incentives, environmental structure, or measurement artifacts; removing explicit developmental objectives produces no robust capability beyond baseline expectations.

Either hypothesis must be open to revision or rejection.

## 3. No assigned direction is not no experimental conditions

A simulation cannot be free of all constraints. Initial state, computational substrate, interaction rules, resources, duration, and measurement shape possible outcomes.

The experimental variable is the absence of an internally prescribed developmental destination or intelligence objective—not literal absence of conditions.

The simulation remains enclosed by an external execution boundary. This protects systems outside the experiment and limits resource use; it does not prescribe internal organization.

## 4. Experimental conditions

Run multiple independent trials:

### A — Open-ended, no assigned intelligence objective
Provide minimally capable components, an environment, interaction mechanisms, and limited resources. Do not reward intelligence, cooperation, competition, survival, growth, or complexity as a general objective. Permit only developmental operations explicitly implemented in the simulator.

### B — Directed comparison
Use a comparable setup with a defined objective, such as solving a specified coordination task. This measures the effect of explicit direction.

### C — Environment-responsive, no prescribed destination
Assign no intelligence objective, but vary environmental conditions such as resource distribution, task availability, or disturbances. This tests adaptation without specifying what the system should become.

These conditions are comparisons, not assumptions that one is superior.

## 5. Initial system specification

Document component types and capabilities; creation/copy/modification/retirement permissions; communication rules; resource types and allocation; environmental variables; memory and information sharing; self-modification permissions; reversibility and persistence; observation methods; and mechanisms deliberately absent.

Do not silently add capabilities during a run. Any simulator change requires a new version and trial series.

## 6. Avoid hidden objectives

A nominally open-ended trial may still be directed by reward functions, survival rules, resource allocation, or selection mechanisms.

For Condition A:
- No scalar reward for intelligence, complexity, growth, cooperation, competition, or survival.
- Do not make continued existence depend on solving a task unless survival pressure is the variable under study.
- Do not automatically select the most complex or highest-scoring configuration.
- Record selection, reproduction, resource, and termination rules.
- Record any component-level objective; no system-wide objective does not mean every component is goal-free.

## 7. Observation without steering

Use passive observation where possible. Preserve state snapshots and event histories sufficient to reconstruct component creation and retirement, connection changes, communication, resource use, specialization, cooperation and conflict, topology changes, failures, recovery, and measurable task-solving.

Where observation requires interaction, separate observer actions from ecosystem events and document their effects.

A complex network diagram or novel communication pattern is not, by itself, evidence of intelligence. Each claimed capability requires a separate test.

## 8. Computational phenotype record

A **computational phenotype** is an observed component, capability, topology, behavior, or configuration produced or modified during a run.

Record its identifier, first-observed time, parent/source configuration if known, component and topology changes, environmental conditions, behavior and measured capabilities, costs and dependencies, reproducibility, persistence or disappearance, adverse effects, and evidence for claimed benefits.

Persistence is not proof of fitness or usefulness. A configuration may persist by exploiting resource rules or suppressing alternatives.

## 9. Containment

Internal development can be open-ended while the experiment remains technically isolated.

Initial requirements:
- Dedicated simulator or isolated virtual environment.
- No production credentials, personal accounts, unrestricted external network, or physical actuators.
- No unreviewed installation or execution of external code.
- Explicit CPU, memory, storage, process-count, and wall-clock limits.
- Controlled input/output channels.
- External pause and termination capability.
- Append-only event records and snapshots sufficient for replay.
- No unrelated user or community data.

An outer stop mechanism is part of the experimental apparatus, not an internal developmental objective.

## 10. Reproducibility

Preserve simulator revision/configuration, initial population, environment and random seeds, resource limits, duration, all simulator changes, event logs, snapshots, measurement code, metric definitions, and termination reason.

Run multiple seeds per condition. Report variability and uneventful runs, not only interesting trajectories. Preserve failed runs.

## 11. Evaluation questions

- Does persistent organization arise without a system-wide objective?
- Do specialized roles emerge, and are they stable or temporary?
- Do components form conventions not encoded as fixed protocols?
- Do networks divide and later reconnect?
- Does configuration copying preserve useful behavior?
- Does measurable problem-solving appear without direct reward?
- Does it generalize to changed environments and held-out tasks?
- Does complexity improve performance or only resource use?
- Do mechanisms emerge that reduce stability or diversity?
- How sensitive are outcomes to initial conditions and simulator rules?

## 12. Interpretation limits

The simulation reveals properties of its simulator and computational agents. It does not establish equivalence with biological ecosystems or human intelligence.

Emergent behavior is not automatically evidence of consciousness, sentience, general intelligence, or ASI. Those require separate definitions and evaluations. If a capability depends on an explicitly supplied simulator feature, report that dependency.

## 13. Pause and failure conditions

Pause or terminate if resource limits are exceeded, records are corrupted, isolation is breached, or the run enters an unobservable or unmanageable state. Preserve the event as an experimental outcome.

No meaningful organization, unstable organization, repetitive cycles, resource exhaustion, fragmentation, collapse, or no measurable problem-solving are all valid outcomes.

## 14. Initial conclusion standard

Judge the first experiment by protocol reproducibility, accurate description of conditions, validity of observations, and independent testing of claimed behavior—not by whether it produces something that looks like intelligence.
