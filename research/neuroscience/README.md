## MNN working definition

**Mesh Neural Networking (MNN)** proposes that computational neurons retain their ordinary processing roles while also being eligible to relay and route signals originating from other neurons, subject to functional-specialization compatibility. Each neuron maintains local awareness of immediate peers and bounded route state rather than a global network map. New neurons are introduced through the relevant specialization domain, without requiring unrelated domains to reorganize.

This is a proposed architecture, not an implemented or validated system. The reviewed literature establishes related components—neuromorphic routing, local coordination, hierarchical event routing, and small-world connectivity—but does not establish the exact MNN combination. See the prior-art note for limitations.

## Terminology and evidence rules

- **Neurogenesis** has an established biological meaning: generation of new neurons from neural stem/progenitor cells. Adult human hippocampal neurogenesis has strong recent molecular evidence, but its magnitude, persistence, and functional contribution remain open research questions.
- **Neural mesh** is used here as an architectural analogy for a dynamically connected computational network. It is not a claim that a HiveNet orchestration mesh is biological tissue.
- **Artificial neurogenesis** is a proposed computational analogy for adding, instantiating, recruiting, or activating computational units. It is not literal neuron birth.
- **Neurosynthesis** is the founder's proposed term for combining separate neurological structures or computational structures into a unified functioning system. It is not presented as a standardized neuroscience term.
- **Mesh Neural Networking (MNN)** is a founder-originated working name for the specialization-aware, locally routed computational neural architecture described in documents 04–07. It is not presented as an established scientific term.
- **Cognitive contagion** is used as an umbrella research phrase. Specific mechanisms such as emotional contagion, social learning, imitation, cultural transmission, and information diffusion should be named precisely when evidence is discussed.
- **MNR-5** is a proposed digital routing experiment: one primary processing path plus five additional independently identifiable input streams. Five is a design constraint for the experiment, not a biological limit or known neuronal capacity.

## Research integrity

Every document distinguishes:
- **Established finding:** supported by peer-reviewed research or authoritative sources.
- **Interpretation:** a reasoned connection or analogy drawn from existing findings.
- **Founder-defined concept:** terminology or conceptual framing supplied by the project owner.
- **Hypothesis/proposal:** a testable idea not yet demonstrated.
- **Open question:** an unresolved scientific or engineering issue.

Digital success does not establish biological feasibility. A computational result may generate a biological hypothesis and candidate design constraints, but translation requires independent biological validation. A preliminary prior-art review is not an exhaustive novelty or patentability assessment.

## Relationship to HiveNet and ENDCAP

HiveNet is the proposed orchestration environment for independently identifiable computational units and dynamically configurable connections. ENDCAP is a potential append-only provenance and experiment-history layer. These are proposed research applications, not claims that the systems currently implement a validated cognitive architecture.

1. Preserve the earlier ANDNM / neural-mesh concept and clarify its terms.
2. Establish a source-backed literature map for cognitive contagion and neural mechanisms.
3. Specify MNR-5 as a digital experiment with measurable criteria.
4. Formalize MNN's local-awareness, specialization, routing, and admission invariants.
5. Build a reproducible simulator and compare MNN against fixed, globally routed, and unrestricted local-mesh baselines.
6. Expand the prior-art review across academic literature, patents, and open-source implementations.
7. Compare successful computational mechanisms with biological constraints.
8. Revise the research hypotheses as evidence accumulates.
