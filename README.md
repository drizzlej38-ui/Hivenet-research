# HiveNet Research

**Status:** Research workspace in active development  
**Maturity:** Conceptual framework, specifications, prototypes, and proposed research directions are distinguished throughout.

## Purpose

This repository is the research and publication workspace for HiveNet: a modular, local-first framework exploring sovereign coordination, opt-in resource sharing, distributed AI, secure messaging, owner-governed data, and resilient human-directed computing.

The repository is intended to support:
- A foundational architecture paper.
- Separate technical papers for major subsystems.
- A traceable engineering and evidence record.
- Literature reviews, source audits, and independent critique.
- Preparation for later archival and scholarly publication.

## Research integrity

This repository does not claim that every described component is implemented, independently validated, novel, secure by proof, or peer reviewed.

Each claim should be identified as one of:
- **Implemented:** present in code, with a reproducible location and evidence.
- **Prototype:** experimentally implemented, with limitations stated.
- **Specified:** described in a design or protocol document but not necessarily implemented.
- **Proposed:** a research direction or hypothesis requiring evaluation.

Test plans are not test results. Hashes establish byte identity, not safety or authorization. Reputation and consensus are not substitutes for cryptographic verification or threat analysis.

## Architectural position under review

Working formulation, pending founder review:

> HiveNet is a modular, local-first coordination and resource-sharing framework. Its current demonstrator uses a central coordinator to register workers and dispatch bounded test tasks over a local network. The longer-term architecture explores federation among independently controlled nodes. In that target architecture, coordination is a bounded role or service rather than an inherent central authority. Participation, resource sharing, external connectivity, and data disclosure are governed by explicit owner- or custodian-authorized policies.

This is a working research statement, not a claim that the full architecture has been implemented.

## Governance and cultural knowledge

Human owners and legitimate community custodians retain authority over participation, access, and sharing. Technical discoverability must not override consent or community governance.

Indigenous and other culturally sensitive knowledge must not be presumed open for publication or redistribution. Applicable community protocols, custodial permissions, and CARE-aligned governance require review before any such material is included.

## Working rule

**Knowledge is not capability. Capability is not command. Authority is not consent.**

The repository will treat that principle as a governance proposition to be examined and operationalized, not as a substitute for detailed technical or legal analysis.
