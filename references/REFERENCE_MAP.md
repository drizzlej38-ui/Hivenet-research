# HiveNet Reference Map

**Status:** Initial bibliography and research map  
**Purpose:** Establish authoritative starting points. Inclusion does not imply that a source validates HiveNet or any particular implementation claim.

## Security architecture and engineering

1. **NIST SP 800-207 — Zero Trust Architecture.** National Institute of Standards and Technology (2020).  
   https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.SP.800-207  
   Relevant to explicit access decisions, resource protection, and avoiding implicit trust based solely on network location. HiveNet-specific owner authority and federation semantics still require their own specification.

2. **NIST SP 800-218 — Secure Software Development Framework (SSDF) v1.1.** NIST (2022).  
   https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.SP.800-218  
   Useful for secure development practices, provenance, vulnerability handling, and evidence discipline.

3. **NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0.** NIST (2024).  
   https://www.nist.gov/cyberframework  
   A risk-management framework for organizing governance, identification, protection, detection, response, and recovery activities.

## Data stewardship and Indigenous governance

4. **Carroll, S. R., et al. (2020). “The CARE Principles for Indigenous Data Governance.” Data Science Journal, 19(1), 43.**  
   https://doi.org/10.5334/dsj-2020-043  
   Introduces Collective Benefit, Authority to Control, Responsibility, and Ethics as principles for Indigenous data governance.

5. **Global Indigenous Data Alliance — CARE Principles.**  
   https://www.gida-global.org/care  
   Background and supporting resources for CARE-aligned data governance.

6. **Wilkinson, M. D., et al. (2016). “The FAIR Guiding Principles for scientific data management and stewardship.” Scientific Data, 3, 160018.**  
   https://doi.org/10.1038/sdata.2016.18  
   FAIR provides guidance on findability, accessibility, interoperability, and reuse. FAIR should not be treated as a substitute for consent, custodianship, or Indigenous governance; CARE and FAIR address complementary concerns.

## Neural systems, cognitive contagion, and biodigital research

7. **Disouky, A., et al. (2026). “Human hippocampal neurogenesis in adulthood, ageing and Alzheimer’s disease.” Nature, 652, 1264–1273.**  
   https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10169-4  
   Multiomic analysis of 355,997 human hippocampal nuclei identifies neural stem cells, neuroblasts, and immature granule neurons across cognitive and age-related cohorts. The study advances molecular evidence for adult human hippocampal neurogenesis while explicitly noting that its functional relevance to cognition remains unknown.

8. **Alonso-Moreno, M. C., et al. (2025). “Human adult hippocampal neurogenesis in health and disease.” Aging Brain, 8, 100145.**  
   https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nbas.2025.100145  
   Review of adult hippocampal neurogenesis, ageing, disease, and methodological considerations.

9. **Loumpourdi, M. (2026). “Do new neurons grow in the adult human hippocampus? A review of the evidence.” Exploration of Neuroscience, 5, 1006128.**  
   https://doi.org/10.37349/en.2026.1006128  
   Reviews conflicting findings and methodological issues in adult human hippocampal neurogenesis. Useful for balanced treatment of uncertainty.

10. **London, M. and Häusser, M. (2005). “Dendritic computation.” Annual Review of Neuroscience, 28, 503–532.**  
    https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.neuro.28.061604.135703  
    Foundational review of linear and nonlinear dendritic computation and synaptic integration.

11. **Payeur, A., Béïque, J.-C., and Naud, R. (2019). “Classes of dendritic information processing.” Current Opinion in Neurobiology, 58, 78–85.**  
    https://doi.org/10.1016/j.conb.2019.07.006  
    Reviews dendritic selection, routing, and multiplexing as proposed and experimentally investigated information-processing functions. Relevant to the MNR-5 inspiration, but does not demonstrate MNR-5.

12. **Fischer, L. F., et al. (2022). “Dendritic Mechanisms for In Vivo Neural Computations and Behavior.” Journal of Neuroscience.**  
    https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.1132-22.2022  
    Reviews dendritic processing, sensory and learning-related computation, and the constraints that biological mechanisms may offer to artificial neural systems.

13. **Chavlis, S. and Poirazi, P. (2021). “Drawing inspiration from biological dendrites to empower artificial neural networks.” Current Opinion in Neurobiology.**  
    https://doi.org/10.1016/j.conb.2021.04.007  
    Reviews how dendritic anatomy, nonlinear integration, and compartmentalized plasticity may inspire more capable or resource-efficient artificial neural networks.

14. **Houran, J., Dagnall, N., Sapkota, R. P., and Lange, R. (2025). “The spread of mind: psychological contagion in theory and critique.” Frontiers in Psychology.**  
    https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1631927  
    Narrative review proposing a multilevel Cascading-Resonance Model of psychological contagion. The authors describe the synthesis as preliminary and call for causal, multilevel research.

15. **Fu, Y., et al. (2025). “The shared neural substrates of emotional mimicry and emotional contagion: an activation likelihood estimation meta-analysis and meta-analytic connectivity modeling analysis.” Social Cognitive and Affective Neuroscience, 20(1), nsaf091.**  
    https://doi.org/10.1093/scan/nsaf091  
    Meta-analysis of shared neural substrates of emotional mimicry and emotional contagion.

16. **Jiang, L., Zou, Q., and Yu, S. (2026). “EEG inter-brain synchrony in social interaction: A systematic review and meta-analysis of condition effects and outcome associations.” Biological Psychology, 210, 109367.**  
    https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsycho.2026.109367  
    Systematic review and meta-analysis of EEG interbrain synchrony in social interaction. Results require interpretation in light of heterogeneity, shared stimuli, behavioral synchrony, and analysis choices.

17. **Brady, W., et al. (2025). “Estimating the effect size of moral contagion in online networks: A pre-registered replication and meta-analysis.” PNAS Nexus.**  
    https://doi.org/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgaf062  
    Examines moral-emotional language and online message diffusion. This concerns social information sharing, not direct neural transmission.

## Research dissemination and citation

18. **GitHub documentation — Referencing and citing content.**  
    https://docs.github.com/en/repositories/archiving-a-github-repository/referencing-and-citing-content  
    Useful for stable references to repository content and commits.

19. **Zenodo documentation — GitHub integration.**  
    https://help.zenodo.org/docs/github/enable-repository/  
    Describes archiving GitHub releases and assigning DOIs. A DOI provides a persistent scholarly reference; it is not peer review, proof of novelty, proof of ownership, or legal protection.

## Further literature required

The bibliography is a starting point, not a completed systematic review. Further work should cover:
- Adult human neurogenesis: conflicting histological, radiocarbon, transcriptomic, and multiomic findings.
- Synaptogenesis, dendritic remodeling, myelination, and plasticity.
- Dendritic computation, neuronal coding, and axonal target selection.
- Interbrain synchrony, social learning, emotional contagion, and causal inference.
- Artificial neural network architectures, mixture-of-experts, modular networks, and weight-merging limitations.
- Neuromorphic engineering, neural interfaces, organoids, and biohybrid systems.
- Digital agent replication, state persistence, provenance, and multi-agent learning.

## Citation practice

For every substantive technical claim:
1. Cite primary research, standards, or authoritative technical documentation where possible.
2. Record publication date and version.
3. Distinguish established findings from interpretation.
4. Explain where a cited source is analogous rather than directly applicable.
5. Avoid implying that a reference validates HiveNet unless the relevant design or experiment has actually been evaluated.

This map is a starting bibliography, not a completed systematic literature review.
